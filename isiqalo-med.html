Isiqalo Med

A secure medical case registry for South African practitioners and their patients. Isiqalo is isiZulu for “the beginning”.

Overview

Isiqalo Med gives every clinical case one secure home.

Practitioners register a case with its condition, treatment, notes and patient details, attach the heavy files that go with it (STL scans, DICOM, X-rays, PDFs and images), and share it with colleagues. Patient identity data is encrypted before it is stored, every read and change is written to a tamper-evident audit log, and records can be exported as JSON, CSV, PDF or ZIP. Patients get their own login to link to, view and download their own records.

It is built for South African healthcare: practitioners register with an HPCSA number and practice number, records carry medical-aid details, and the privacy design follows POPIA and the HPCSA record-keeping guidelines.

The problem it solves

A patient’s scans live in an inbox, their notes on a USB stick and their history in a chat app.

Clinical cases, especially ones with imaging and 3D scan files, end up scattered. That makes them hard to find, hard to share safely and hard to account for when something goes wrong. Isiqalo Med puts the case, its files, the conversation about it and the record of who saw it in one place.

Who it is for

  • PractitionersRegister, then wait for an administrator to verify their HPCSA number before they can touch patient data. They see only the patients assigned to them.
  • PatientsLink to their records with ID number, surname and date of birth, then view them, download their data or ask for their account to be deleted.
  • AdministratorsVerify practitioners, see all records and check the integrity of the audit log.
  • ViewersRead-only access to de-identified records, with no documents.

Key features

  • Case registration with filesOrganisation, facility type, one of 21 medicine categories, condition, treatment and notes, plus up to 10 files per upload (STL, DICOM, X-ray, PDF, images) of up to 250 MB each.
  • Encryption at restNames, ID numbers, contact details, medical-aid numbers, notes and history are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. A keyed lookup hash finds ID numbers without decrypting every row.
  • Role-based accessPer-record authorisation checks the caller’s role and their link to each patient, tested by an automated role-by-endpoint matrix.
  • CollaborationCase comments and private messages between colleagues and their own patients.
  • Data extractionExport selected patients as JSON, CSV, PDF with embedded images, or a ZIP with a clinical PDF report and the raw files.
  • Tamper-evident audit logEvery read and change of patient data is logged in an append-only, SHA-256 hash-chained table that admins can verify.
  • Consent and patient rights (POPIA)Recorded patient consent on every new record, a versioned privacy notice, opt-in marketing, “download my data” and account deletion requests.
  • Secure sign-inPasswords of 12 or more characters, per-account lockout after 5 failures, optional two-factor authentication (TOTP) and 1-hour sessions re-checked on every request.
  • Safe document linksFiles are streamed through signed links that expire after 10 minutes, never through raw storage URLs.
  • Retention and backupsSoft delete with destruction after the HPCSA retention period, and encrypted, rotated backups with a tested restore.

My role

I led Isiqalo Med from planning through to a working product.

  • Gathered requirements and planned the work around South African rules: HPCSA registration, medical aids and POPIA.
  • Designed the interface and built the React front end with views for sign-in, dashboard, case upload, browsing, management, extraction, messages and settings.
  • Built the Express and TypeScript API, the SQLite data model and the file storage layer.
  • Hardened it for security and privacy, then tested it: 167 automated API checks and 41 keyboard and axe-core accessibility checks run in CI.
  • Wrote the documentation, including the compliance map and every configurable limit.

Codebase structure

A layered back end (routes, controllers, repositories, then the database and services) and a single-page React app, shipped together in one Docker image behind an nginx proxy.

Around 124 files in the repository, all application code in TypeScript.

Challenges and lessons learned

Compliance is a process, not a feature

Code can provide the technical controls POPIA expects and the evidence for them, but registering an Information Officer, legal review and staff training sit outside the code. I learnt to say plainly what is done and what is still outstanding.

Concurrency on a single-file database

SQLite is simple to run, but concurrent requests could interleave inside one transaction. A serialised transaction queue and WAL journaling solved it.

Large files without large memory

Scans can be hundreds of megabytes, so files stream from storage straight to the response or into the ZIP, and every export has caps on size, time and concurrency.

Security details that are easy to miss

Login timing is equalised so it does not reveal which accounts exist, CSV exports neutralise formula injection, and signed file tokens are redacted from logs.

Next steps on the roadmap: mandatory two-factor authentication for admins, encrypting the remaining patient fields, key rotation tooling, alerting, and an in-browser viewer for DICOM and STL files.
